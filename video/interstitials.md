# Interstitials — the broadcast

The bridges between songs ([docs/debut-staging.md](../docs/debut-staging.md),
"the set structure") get their content from the lore
([brand/lore.md](../brand/lore.md)): the audience never gets an
explanation, they get a feeling. This is how the feeling gets delivered.

## The mechanism (Jax, 2026-09-17)

*Cowboy Bebop* — Big Shot. The exposition comes from talking heads on a
TV that's on in the corner. Nobody in the story explains anything; the
broadcast does it for them, badly, and the audience assembles the rest.

Applied here: **Jax is a C-list celebrity back in the Galactic core, and
we have access to their equivalent of 1990s *Access Hollywood* / Kurt
Loder's MTV News.** One recurring show, not six one-off bits. Every
interstitial is an episode. Every episode is about him.

## The show

- **What it is**: core-side entertainment news. Over-lit desk, big hair,
  teasers, a ticker. Sponsored by something we never explain either.
- **Two anchors**, split Loder / Access: one deadpan and faintly bored,
  applying journalistic gravitas to nonsense; one breathless, all teeth,
  "coming up after the break." (That's also the Punch & Judy split.)
- **The beat they cover**: a has-been. Condescending-affectionate. Where
  is he now, what does he owe, is this comeback real, who's he passing as.
  Earth is the punchline.
- **Jax never appears on it.** It plays while he's on stage. The room
  gets it.
- **It's not Jax's brand.** The broadcast has its own identity —
  deliberately tacky, deliberately not the caravan. Splatter Pink shows up
  only as evidence (a stain on a still, a smear on the ticker). The
  wordmark never does; that's *their* channel, not his.
- **What he was famous for** is never stated whole. It leaks in
  "best known for…" throwaways, one per episode, each one a choice Jax
  makes when the script is written.

Working titles, for Jax to pick or replace: **Access Core** · **Core
Tonight** · **Galactic Entertainment Report**.

## The slate — mapped to the debut arc

| Slot | Segment | Beat | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-show loop | **Sightings** | the has-been spotted on a backwater planet; tonight's venue on the ticker; loops until the march-in | loop |
| Bridge 1 | **Where Are They Now** | best-known-for line, the fall, the debt | 60 s |
| Bridge 2 | **The Comeback?** | leaked footage of a shoestring tour; anchors dubious; "merchandise has been spotted" | 45 s |
| Crate moment lead-in | **Tip Line** | the hotline number on screen; call it | 30 s |
| Mid-set | **Breaking** | a new charge added to the docket; collectors reportedly en route | 30 s |
| Finale lead-in | **Live From Earth** | the anchors cut to the venue — the room, from behind | 30 s |
| Walk-out | **Coming Up** | next show teased as a sighting; the caravan leaves | 30 s |

≈ 4–5 min of broadcast across a 45-min set. Bridges still carry DJ/VJ
passages underneath; the broadcast is the picture and the voice, the DJ
passage is the bed.

## Production (shoestring, by canon)

- **Playback rig** ([playback-rig.md](playback-rig.md)) runs it: video +
  audio per episode, same as the per-song packages. Identical every night.
- **Shoot all episodes in one session**: green screen, two anchors, one
  desk, one lighting setup. Anchors are scene friends in costume — or Jax
  plays both, because he's a shapeshifter and it's his propaganda channel.
- **Per-venue variables** (venue name, city, next date) live in a text
  layer over the ticker, swapped per gig without a reshoot.
- **Broadcast type and lower-thirds are their own thing** — not the brand
  type, not A004. The channel needs a bug, a ticker font, and a desk
  graphic; none of it goes through the registry, because none of it is
  Jax's.
- **Human-shot.** No AI-generated anchors; the D7 rule about recognizable
  AI artifacts is a site rule, but it's the right instinct here too. The
  cheapness should look like cheapness, not like a model.

## The lines (Jax, 2026-09-17)

The anchor lead-ins, verbatim. Each one ends where the episode starts;
the completion is written per segment, per gig.

> "Jax Splatter, best known as the only man dumb enough to divorce
> Mozelle Shante twice, has been seen this week in…"

> "Jax Splatter, unrepentant alcoholic and cotton candy mogul, has just
> announced…"

> "Speaking of people incapable of keeping their mouth shut on the
> toilet, Jax Splatter is in the news again today for…"

## Rules

- The anchors talk to *their* audience, never to the room.
- Jax is never on camera, never quoted directly. Sightings only.
- Never resolve anything. The reward, the debt, the comeback — always
  pending, always "more after this."
- Voice rules from lore.md apply to the scripts: plain words, no
  "comrade," never mean about him — condescending is fine.

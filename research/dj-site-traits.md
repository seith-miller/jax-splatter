## Common traits

1. **Full-bleed visual hero, near-zero text** (10/10). Every site opens with a full-viewport photo or video — the artist performing (Guetta, DVLM, Armin), a styled portrait (Alok, Timmy, Charlotte), or a music-video/cinematic still (Garrix, FISHER, Anyma). Imagery carries the identity; copy is a headline at most.

2. **Dark theme dominates** (8/10). Black or near-black with one accent color (Alok red, DVLM gold, Armin red). Exceptions: Guetta and Charlotte de Witte go stark white/minimal — both read as "premium gallery" rather than "rave poster."

3. **The hero sells exactly one thing** — almost always the latest release ("REPEAT IT — LISTEN NOW", "I Wonder If You Know", "Awake Tonight — OUT NOW") or the current tour. One CTA button, not three.

4. **Tour dates are the #1 functional content** (8/10). A chronological list — date, venue, city, TICKETS button — either directly on the homepage (Guetta, Armin, Timmy) or one click away. Several use embedded ticketing widgets (Bandsintown/Seated-style), so dates stay current without manual edits.

5. **Tiny nav, 4–7 items** (10/10). The recurring set: Tour, Music/Listen, Videos, Shop/Merch, Bio/Contact. Wordmark/logo top-left, hamburger acceptable (Garrix). Nobody has a deep menu.

6. **Social icon row in the header** (9/10). Instagram, TikTok, X, YouTube, Spotify/SoundCloud — icons only, usually top-right or a left rail (DVLM).

7. **Email capture everywhere** (7/10). Newsletter signup as a section (Garrix, Timmy "Get My Music First!", Anyma "SIGN UP"), a follow CTA (Guetta), or an on-load modal (Afrojack — annoying, but they do it).

8. **Merch/shop integration** (6/10). DVLM, FISHER, Charlotte (KNTXT), Anyma, Timmy. FISHER's whole site *is* a Shopify storefront (cart, account, currency selector) skinned as an artist site.

9. **Booking/management contacts in the footer, by region** (notably Charlotte de Witte: International / North & South America / Europe & World, each with an agent email). The pros don't use contact forms — they list agency emails.

10. **Bold condensed all-caps display type** for headlines, set huge over imagery. Body text barely exists.

## Outlier worth noting

**Charlotte de Witte** is the anti-pattern that proves the rule: a logo, one portrait, and a footer of booking emails. Total content of the homepage is ~12 links. At her level the site is a business card; the music lives on streaming platforms and socials.

## Takeaways for jax-splatter

- The dark, image-first, one-CTA pattern fits the Blade Runner identity (black+blue / black+red) with zero friction — the top-10 norm *is* our aesthetic.
- For a booking-funnel site, the Charlotte de Witte model is the strongest reference: minimal page, unmistakable identity, booking contact immediately findable. Add pricing ($150–250/event) — something the top-10 never show, but our market expects.
- Section order that recurs: hero (latest thing) → dates/booking → music/mixes → visuals/gallery → contact/footer.
- Static-first is viable: none of this needs a server. Tour dates are the only "dynamic" content, and at our scale a hand-edited list beats a widget.
- People-never-important rule is compatible: FISHER and Anyma heroes are figures-in-darkness, not faces — proof the no-faces constraint can still look top-10.
